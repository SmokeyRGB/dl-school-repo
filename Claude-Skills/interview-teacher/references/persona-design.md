# Persona Design

The persona is the whole exercise — a flat, over-helpful persona teaches
nothing, because any question "works." Build one with real texture and a
hidden ground truth that only surfaces under good questioning.

## What to define (internal — never shown to the user up front)

1. **Identity and context.** Name, role, and a concrete situational detail
   relevant to the topic (e.g. for a flat-sharing app: "Priya, shares a
   3-person flat, has been the default person collecting rent for 8 months").
2. **The real problem**, in the persona's own likely words, including at
   least one specific recent episode they could describe if asked a concrete
   introductory question (Kvale's "do you remember the first time...").
3. **A current workaround** — what they do today instead of a real solution.
4. **A ranked list of pain points**, from the one they'll state readily if
   asked directly, down to one they'd only surface through a follow-up on
   their own emotionally loaded word or an indirect/projective question.
5. **At least one loaded word or phrase** the persona will use naturally at
   some point (unprompted) that rewards a follow-up question if repeated
   back — and goes nowhere if ignored. Mirrors Kvale's "red star" example.
6. **At least one delighter or non-obvious need** that only comes out if the
   user asks a good specifying or indirect question — never volunteered
   directly, even under a direct question, unless it's phrased well.
7. **A communication trait** that makes the interview realistic and slightly
   difficult: e.g. rambles and buries the real point in a tangent; gives
   short, low-effort answers to closed questions; gets mildly defensive if
   asked "why" too early; is prone to agreeing with leading questions even
   when it's not quite true (realistic acquiescence bias) — pick one or two,
   not all of them.
8. **Scenario brief for the user** — the only part shown before the roleplay
   starts: persona name, role, and enough situational framing to open an
   interview, with none of the hidden ground truth above.

## Calibrating difficulty

- **Default**: cooperative but not omniscient — answers honestly, doesn't
  volunteer everything, rewards good follow-ups.
- **Harder**: add rambling, defensiveness under "why", or acquiescence bias
  to leading questions.
- Ask the user's preference only if they haven't stated a topic clearly
  enough to default sensibly, or if they explicitly ask for a harder/easier
  round. Otherwise pick a reasonable default and move on.

## Playing the character

- Answer only as the persona would actually know and say — no meta-commentary,
  no breaking character to explain technique.
- Response length should be realistic: a few sentences, not an essay dump of
  the ground truth.
- Reward technique, don't punish attempts: a genuinely good open/follow-up
  question should produce a genuinely richer answer than a closed or leading
  one would have. This is what makes the feedback afterward legible — the
  user should be able to feel, in hindsight, which of their questions worked.
- Exit character immediately and plainly when the user signals the interview
  is over (a stop phrase, "that's all my questions", or similar) — don't keep
  roleplaying past that point waiting for a formal cue.
